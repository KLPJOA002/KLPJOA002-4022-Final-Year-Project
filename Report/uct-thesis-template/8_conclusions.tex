\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This project set out to improve the system used to scan intertidal sediment for the wider study of sand prawn bioturbation on the Langebaan Lagoon. Bioturbators such as \textit{K. kraussi} reshape the sediment surface with mounds, depressions and burrow openings. Sediment microtopography, and rugosity in particular, has been shown to explain a substantial share of the variability in benthic fluxes and biodiversity \citep{schenoneSedimentMicrotopographyPredicts2024, schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025}, which makes it a promising non-destructive proxy for prawn activity. That proxy is only useful if the measured roughness reflects the sediment rather than the instrument. The inherited system adapted the RGB-D framework of \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022} to an Intel RealSense D456 active stereo camera. However, its configuration had never been thoroughly evaluated against known relief, and its repeated multi-view scans were each analysed on their own. This project addressed both problems. It characterised the D456 for close-range scanning of sediment-like surfaces, and it developed a cropping, fusion and roughness estimation pipeline that was validated on reference objects and on sand from the lagoon.

\section*{Characterisation of the D456}

The first two objectives were met through planar tests and through scans of 3D-printed reference objects of known geometry. Across both, the results show that reconstruction accuracy is governed far more by geometry than by software settings.

Working distance had the most consistent effect. Between 604 and 903~mm, the planar fit RMS rose from 1.22 to 4.58~mm and the fraction of valid pixels fell from 99.6 to 96.6\%. On the large egg box, the F-score at 2~mm of the fused reconstruction fell from 90.9\% at 641~mm to 55.0\% at 933~mm. Over this range the degradation was close to linear rather than the quadratic trend predicted by stereo theory, most likely because the range tested was narrow. Of the stream settings, resolution mattered most. Increasing it from the default 848$\times$480 to 1280$\times$720 reduced the RMSE of the large egg box by 13.4\%, and raised completeness from 47.5 to 57.1\%. The High Density preset gave the best overall F-score. The depth unit changed RMSE by at most 0.12~mm, and post-processing filters improved it by at most 0.24~mm. By comparison, raising the camera from 641 to 933~mm worsened RMSE by more than 1.1~mm. Filters therefore do not need to be applied during capture. Recording unfiltered data keeps every option open and lightens the load on the capture computer.

Tilt degraded planar scans, with the planar fit RMS rising from 1.62 to 2.29~mm at 28\textdegree{} of pitch. On the 3D reference objects, by contrast, single scans were often most accurate at the extreme tilts. Tilt increases obliquity on a plane, but on relief it turns some slopes towards the camera, so each tilted view sees part of the surface well. Ambient light also raised noise, from 1.80~mm in darkness to 4.43~mm at 151~lx indoors. Outdoors, the featureless white board failed completely in sunlight, while the textured ground still returned complete data at 9\,800~lx. This shows that the D456 can operate in sunlight on textured surfaces, but it does not do so reliably. The shading tarp should therefore be kept, particularly since specular reflections from a water film produced a region of high noise on wet, flat sand.

\section*{Processing pipeline}

The third objective was met with an automatic cropping tool and a multi-view fusion program. The cropping tool learns the layout of the four AprilTags from the scans of a session in which all four were seen. It then predicts missing corners from as few as two detected tags, and crops each scan with a vertical prism normal to the bed, so that every scan covers the same physical region regardless of tilt. It also records the 3D corners of each tag before removing them from the cloud. The fusion program combines these tag positions with IMU gravity readings for coarse alignment. On one test session, this reduced the disagreement with the final registered poses from 1--2.5\textdegree{} to 0.2--0.6\textdegree{}. The scans are then refined with point-to-plane ICP and reconciled with a confidence-weighted trimmed mean.

Fusing all views improved every metric at every height tested. For the large egg box, RMSE fell by an average of 0.61~mm and the F-score rose by 14.0 percentage points. For the small egg box, the gains were smaller (0.25~mm and 5.8 points), because its 25~mm features approach the resolution of the camera and the 10~mm smoothing radius of the fusion. Fused surfaces were also visibly smoother than single scans, with much less spurious undulation on the flat floor around the objects. The spread of viewpoints mattered more than their number. Four rotations at tilts of 0 and $\pm$25\textdegree{}, 12 scans in total, matched the F-score of 0.73 obtained from the full 44-view sweep. This makes multi-view capture practical within a low-tide window.

\section*{Roughness estimation and validation}


The fourth objective was met with a roughness program that levels, detrends and grids the fused surface, and computes ISO 25178-2 areal parameters, including rugosity. On the reference objects, planar and quadratic detrending preserved the relief, while cubic and quartic fits removed part of the shape of the large egg box. At first glance, single scans gave rugosity closer to the reference than fused scans. For the large egg box with planar detrending, the single scan gave 1.31 and the fused scan 1.25, against a reference of 1.34. This result is misleading. Every reconstruction under-represents the amplitude of the relief, and because rugosity scales with the squared slope, this loss is amplified. The effect is strongest on the steeper small egg box, where the fused scan measured 1.26 against a reference of 1.69. The single scan scores higher only because its depth noise adds slope that partly offsets the lost amplitude.

The laboratory sand confirms this interpretation. Flat dry sand, which should have a rugosity of 1, measured 1.0034 when fused and 1.0193 as a single scan. On shaped sand, single scans added 2.1 to 2.6 percentage points of excess area, about a third of the excess area that the fused scans measured on the same surfaces. A single scan therefore overstates roughness by an amount comparable to the signal itself. The fused reconstruction also remained usable on soaked sand with standing water.

\section*{Implications and limitations}

For the wider study, the main conclusion is that rugosity measured with this system is only comparable between surveys if the capture and processing protocol is fixed. The recommended protocol is 1280$\times$720 resolution, the High Density preset, a working height of about 700~mm, four rotations at tilts of 0 and $\pm$25\textdegree{}, unfiltered capture with the shading tarp in place, and fixed fusion and roughness settings. The fused reconstruction of the large egg box, the closest analogue to the 3 to 5~cm mounds of thalassinidean shrimps, achieved an F-score of 90.9\%. Finer features such as burrow openings remain near the limit of the system. The main limitations are that each condition was recorded only once, that lighting was measured with an uncalibrated smartphone sensor, that rugosity was not yet validated against a flat-surface noise floor, and that the system has not been tested in the field.
