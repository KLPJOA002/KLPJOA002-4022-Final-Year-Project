\subsection{Biological Activity}
\label{subsec:ch1.section1.subsec1}
%----------------------------------------------------
Sand prawn activity has a large effect on the surrounding ecosystem. Pillay and Branch \cite{inbook} explain that by physically burrowing into the sediment, they distribute buried sediment to the surface, alter the surface structure via the introduced mounds, which can increase surface area, and alter both chemical and structural properties of the sediment through burrowing and nutrient mixing. Venter et al \cite{venterWaterFiltrationBurrowing2020} further shows that sand prawn bi-directional water pumping filters can reduce phytoplankton biomass by up to 70\% in estuarine environments which can help with ecosystem resilience.
%The microtopography of sediment surface in inter-coastal regions is strongly linked to the bio-diversity (cite) and bio-density within the benthic ecosystem. Specifically, sand prawn population within the Langebaan lagoon are strong members in the bioturbation industry and play a large part in moving sediment around, which alters the physical structure of the sediment, and the chemical properties therein. Measuring and analysing the microtopography presents itself as a strong measurement for the bio-density and activity of sand prawns in these regions.

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:ch1.problemstatement}
%====================================================
Currently, The system makes use of a single scan to estimate surface roughness. And whilst each surface is scanned at least four times, using the original camera mount which allowed rotations, these repetitions were not combined together and each was evaluated individually. Further, the current configuration of the system, both in terms of hardware and software, has not been thoroughly investigated. This project aims to provide a comprehensive characterisation of the Intel RealSense D456 camera, and provide a method for high quality three dimensional reconstruction and analysis using multi-view scans.

\section{Project objectives}
\label{sec:ch1.projectobjectives}
%====================================================
%The \gls{gps} is used for positioning. The \gls{miz} is of particular interest in the \gls{so}.
The objectives of this project are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Characterise the Intel RealSense D456 camera across different external configurations such as: height, angle and lighting
	
	\item Characterise the Intel RealSense D456 camera under different firmware and software configurations, such as: Image Resolutions, depth units, presets, and filter combinations
	
	\item Develop a multi-view fusion process to combine different view point scans into a single scan, and characterise this process against different combinations of tilts and rotations
	
	\item Develop a roughness metric evaluation pipeline to process fused scans and obtain surface roughness metrics required for the overarching project.
	
	\item Develop a feature detection process to help record useful sediment features such as burrows. 
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope, limitations and assumptions}
\label{sec:ch1.scope}
%====================================================
The project is limited to a duration of only 12 weeks, with the final submission on the 27th of October 2026. The project is limited to the hardware present on the current existing system. This includes an Intel RealSense D456 camera, a rotating camera mount, staged on top of a servo operated tilt stage. 

This project is primarily focused on the optimisation of the Intel RealSense D456 camera for operation on intertidal sediment surfaces. The resulting configuration analysis are primarily oriented for resolving features present in this environment, and therefore the process may not be applicable for all reconstruction objectives, such as angular objects with sharp corners.

\section{Plan of development}
\label{sec:ch1.planofdevelopment}

Plan of development involves the following:

Firstly, the existing capture pipeline is altered to interface with the tilt stage servo motor, to automate the tilt stage for capture. 

Secondly, a cropping tool is to be made to automatically crop the scaned region into a known defined region for analysis.

Thirdly, a fusion tool is to be made. This will take multi-view scans taken of each surface, align them, and then fuse them into one higher quality reconstruction of the surface.

Fourthly, a metric evaluation tool is to be made to analyse the resulting surfaces and produce roughness metrics for them.

lastly, a feature detection and segmentation tool is made to automatically characterise and count surface features such as burrows.
