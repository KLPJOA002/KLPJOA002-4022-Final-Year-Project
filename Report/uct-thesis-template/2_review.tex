%\section{Sand prawn bioturbation and its ecological significance}
%\label{sec:review.bioturbation}
%%=====================================================================
%\citet{kristensenWhatBioturbationNeed2012} argue that bioturbation needs a precise definition that separates its components, mainly the reworking of sediment particles and the ventilation of burrows, so that each can be measured and compared across studies. Burrowing thalassinidean shrimps are among the most intense bioturbators. In their review, \citet{inbook} describe them as ecosystem engineers. By excavating burrows and ejecting sediment at the surface they alter grain size, organic content, porewater chemistry and surface topography, and these changes propagate to other fauna and flora. The effects of this activity on the surface is predominantly in the form of mounds, depressions and burrow openings. The review reports mounds of about 5.4\,cm in height and 11.7\,cm in width for \textit{Callianassa subterranea} in the North Sea, and of about 3\,cm in height and 15\,cm in width for \textit{Pestarella candida} in the Mediterranean. These values indicate the scale of relief a measurement system must resolve,  centimetre-scale heights over decimetre-scale wavelengths.
%
%On the South African coast the dominant species is the sand prawn \textit{Callichirus kraussi}, named \textit{Callianassa kraussi} in the older literature. In Langebaan Lagoon, \citet{nelAssessmentAbundanceDistribution2013} found that it occupied most of the intertidal area and dominated its sandy sediments, and that its distribution followed sediment characteristics. Its engineering effects are substantial. \citet{branchImpactSandPrawn1987} reported sediment turnover of the order of 12\,kg of wet sediment per square metre per day, which buried benthic microalgae and altered bacterial and meiofaunal communities. In caging experiments, \citet{pillayExperimentalEvidenceEffects2007} found that excluding sand prawns increased the abundance of surface grazers and of both suspension-feeding and deposit-feeding polychaetes and bivalves, while burrowing infauna were unaffected. \citet{venterWaterFiltrationBurrowing2020} extended the role of the species to the water column. Bi-directional pumping through the burrows, with adsorption of phytoplankton onto the burrow walls, reduced phytoplankton biomass by roughly 50\,\% in experiments, and long-term estuarine data showed reductions of roughly 70\,\% in prawn-dominated areas, which suggests that sand prawns help to buffer coastal ecosystems against eutrophication.
%
%Because these effects scale with prawn density and activity, understanding them requires the activity to be measured. The usual approaches are sediment cores, which are destructive and slow to process, and counts of burrow openings, which are laborious and capture only one kind of surface feature. Surface structure is also ecologically important in its own right: \citet{thrushFishingDisturbanceMarine2001} showed that disturbances which homogenise surficial sediments and reduce habitat structure can have important consequences for soft-sediment biodiversity. The relief of the sediment surface is therefore both a product of bioturbation and a habitat variable, which raises the question of whether it can be measured in place of the animals themselves.

%The case for microtopography as a proxy rests mainly on a sequence of studies by Schenone and colleagues. Their rugosity was obtained from the sediment profile imaged through the tank wall, a single two-dimensional slice of the surface. \citet{schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025} took the approach into the field, reconstructing the seafloor of several subtidal habitats with structure-from-motion (SfM) photogrammetry, and found that microtopography consistently explained more than half of the variance in environmental variables, benthic fluxes, biodiversity metrics and functional traits.

The studies were subtidal, in New Zealand, and involved different species, so the relationship has not been demonstrated for \textit{C.~kraussi} on exposed intertidal flats. The relationships are also correlative as they establish microtopography as a surrogate rather than as a mechanism. Testing the ecological relationship at Langebaan is the purpose of the wider study to which this project contributes. The task here is to provide measurements accurate enough for that test.

%How roughness is quantified matters as much as whether it is measured. Benthic ecology traditionally uses chain-and-tape rugosity, the ratio of the length of a chain laid along the contours of the bed to the straight line distance between its ends. \citet{friedmanMultiScaleMeasuresRugosity2012} note that this samples a single profile and tends to overestimate rugosity when the chain wanders sideways. They instead computed rugosity from stereo-derived meshes as surface area relative to the area projected onto a plane of best fit, which decouples rugosity from slope and allows it to be evaluated over windows of different sizes. \citet{storlazziEndChainRugosity2016} derived rugosity from SfM reconstructions of existing reef video and found it more effective and quantitatively powerful than chain-and-tape. Surface metrology provides a standardised vocabulary for the same quantities. ISO 25178-2 defines areal height, spatial and hybrid parameters on a scale-limited surface \citep{ISO2517822021,leachCharacterisationArealSurface2013}, of which the developed interfacial area ratio $S_{dr}$ is rugosity expressed as a percentage excess area (\autoref{ch:theory}). \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022} adopted a comparable set, computing nine morphological characteristics, including arithmetic mean roughness, mean peak height and rugosity, from detrended depth images.
%
%These parameters are not independent of how the surface is sampled. \citet{jacobsQuantitativeCharacterizationSurface2017} show that common roughness parameters depend on the scan size and pixel resolution chosen by the researcher, and recommend the power spectral density as a description that is not biased by these choices. Slope-based parameters such as $S_{dq}$, $S_{dr}$ and rugosity are dominated by the shortest resolved wavelengths, where sensor noise and smoothing also act (\autoref{ch:theory}). Rugosity from a depth camera is therefore only interpretable when the noise floor and effective resolution of the measurement are known. Without that, a change in rugosity cannot be attributed to the sediment rather than to the instrument.

%\section{Non-contact measurement of sediment surfaces}
%\label{sec:review.methods}
%%=====================================================================
%A system for this application must resolve millimetre-scale relief over patches of a few tenths of a square metre, be portable and fast enough to use within a low-tide window, leave the surface undisturbed, and operate in daylight on dry, wet and waterlogged sand. Coring and contact profiling fail the third requirement because they damage the fragile structures being measured \citep{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022}. The non-contact options differ mainly in cost, processing effort and tolerance of sunlight.
%
%Terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) is accurate and is used on sandy coasts, where its return intensity has even been calibrated against surface moisture \citep{noletMeasuringModelingEffect2014}, but the instruments are expensive and the data cumbersome to process \citep{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022}. SfM photogrammetry is inexpensive in hardware and can reach spatial resolutions orders of magnitude finer than large-scale lidar or sonar surveys \citep{storlazziEndChainRugosity2016}, which is why it underpins the field work of \citet{schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025}. It does, however, require many overlapping images, an external scale reference and offline processing, so the result is not available while still on site.
%
%Consumer depth cameras offer immediate, metrically scaled depth at low cost. \citet{mankoffKinectLowcostHighresolution2013} introduced the structured-light Kinect to the Earth sciences, reporting depth resolution of 1\,mm or better at 0.5\,m, degrading to about 75\,mm at 5\,m, and demonstrating it in glaciology, stream bathymetry and geomorphology. \citet{kamalInvestigatingThreedimensionalMesoscale2014} used the same sensor to quantify the three-dimensional complexity of mangrove roots. \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022}, the framework on which this project builds, used an Asus Xtion Pro Live inside a semi-opaque container to image 0.3\,m$^2$ patches of intertidal sediment at seven sites (82 surfaces). They first tested four variants of a geometric detrending model on simulated surfaces, then applied the two best-performing models, linear and quadratic, to the field data, and found strong agreement between the two in the extracted surface measures. The container was a necessity rather than a choice. A structured-light camera triangulates against its own projected pattern, which sunlight washes out, so shading fixed both the size of each scan and its single viewpoint.
%
%Sunlight tolerance therefore decides the choice of sensor. Structured-light and time-of-flight (ToF) cameras rely entirely on their own near-infrared illumination. An active stereo camera instead matches texture between two infrared imagers, and its projected dot pattern only supplements the scene texture. On a naturally textured surface in strong light it degrades towards a passive stereo camera rather than failing outright \citep{keselmanIntelRRealSenseTMStereoscopic2017,ProjectorsD400Series}. \citet{vitComparingRGBDSensors2018} tested four sensors outdoors at several distances and lighting conditions: a ToF Kinect v2, a structured-light Orbbec Astra S, a coded-light Intel SR300 and an active stereo Intel D435. They concluded that the D435 was the most viable for close-range field measurements. Purely passive stereo cameras such as the ZED 2 also tolerate sunlight but require a GPU-equipped host to compute depth \citep{rustlerEmpiricalComparisonFour2025}, whereas the RealSense D400 cameras compute depth on board. The D456 used here is the IP65-rated variant of the D455 \citep{intel_d456}, which also suits wet intertidal work. Active stereo is thus the best-supported low-cost option, but the evidence for it comes from other camera models and other surfaces.

%\section{Registration and fusion of multi-view depth scans}
%\label{sec:review.fusion}
%%=====================================================================
%Combining depth scans from several viewpoints is well established in computer vision. \citet{curlessVolumetricMethodBuilding1996} integrated aligned range images into a cumulative weighted signed distance function, which averages noise, fills gaps and weights measurements by their reliability. KinectFusion \citep{newcombeKinectFusionRealtimeDense2011} applied the same representation in real time to a moving consumer depth camera, tracking each frame against the growing model with ICP, and showed that the fused model contains detail absent from any single frame. \citet{nguyenModelingKinectSensor2012} improved on this by weighting measurements with a sensor noise model. These methods were developed for objects and rooms, and their quality has been judged by geometric accuracy rather than by the roughness parameters derived from the result.
%
%Two further results bear on sediment. First, nearly flat surfaces are a degenerate case for registration. \citet{gelfandGeometricallyStableSampling2003} show that ICP cannot constrain sliding within a plane or rotation about its normal, so a near-planar bed needs external constraints such as fiducial markers, for which AprilTags \citep{olsonAprilTagRobustFlexible2011,wangAprilTag2Efficient2016} are a robust choice, or inertial measurements. Second, in benthic mapping, fusion is normally done at survey scale. \citet{friedmanMultiScaleMeasuresRugosity2012} built rugosity maps from stereo imagery registered with vehicle navigation, rather than by fusing repeated close-range views of a single patch. \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022} analysed each surface from a single fixed viewpoint inside the container, and the inherited system for this project likewise analysed each of its repeated scans individually. No study was found that fuses multi-view depth scans of a sediment patch and quantifies how the choice of views affects the accuracy of the reconstruction and of the roughness parameters derived from it.

\section{Sand prawn bioturbation and its ecological significance}
\label{sec:review.bioturbation}
%=====================================================================
Bioturbation is the reworking of sediment particles and the ventilation of burrows by animals \citep{kristensenWhatBioturbationNeed2012}. Burrowing thalassinidean shrimps are among the most intense bioturbators in marine soft-sediment environments \citep{cadeeSedimentDynamicsBioturbating2001,rowdenRoleCallianassaSubterranea1998} and are thus deemed ecosystem engineers, as they modify their physical surroundings affecting their co-occurring ecological community \citep{inbook}. Excavating burrows and ejecting sediment alters grain size, organic content, porewater chemistry and surface topography \citet{inbook}. At the surface this appears as mounds, depressions and burrow openings (\autoref{fig:review.prawn}). Reported mounds are about 3 to 5.4\,cm high and 12 to 15\,cm wide \citep{inbook}, so a measurement system must resolve centimetre-scale heights over decimetre-scale wavelengths.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\litfig{figs/ch2/sandprawn_mounds.jpg}{0.6\linewidth}
	\caption{Surface Mounds and Burrow Openings Produced by \textit{K. kraussi} on an Intertidal Sandflat. Taken on 14/07/2026 During a Field Trip}
	\label{fig:review.prawn}
\end{figure}

On the South African coast the dominant species is the sand prawn \textit{K. kraussi} (\textit{Callianassa kraussi} in older literature), which occupies most of the intertidal area of Langebaan Lagoon \citep{nelAssessmentAbundanceDistribution2013}. Its effects are substantial:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
	\item Sediment turnover of about 12\,kg\,m$^{-2}$ of wet sediment per day, burying benthic microalgae and altering bacterial and meiofaunal communities \citep{branchImpactSandPrawn1987};
	
	\item Increased abundance of surface grazers and of suspension- and deposit-feeding polychaetes and bivalves when prawns were excluded \citep{pillayExperimentalEvidenceEffects2007};
	
	\item Reductions in phytoplankton biomass of about 50\,\% in experiments and 70\,\% in prawn-dominated estuarine areas, through pumping water across the burrow walls, which helps buffer coastal ecosystems against eutrophication \citep{venterWaterFiltrationBurrowing2020}.
\end{itemize}

Measuring this activity usually relies on sediment cores, which are destructive and slow, or on counts of burrow openings, which are laborious and capture only one surface feature. Surface structure also matters in its own right, as homogenising it reduces soft-sediment biodiversity \citep{thrushFishingDisturbanceMarine2001}. Surface relief is therefore both a product of bioturbation and a habitat variable, which raises the question of whether it can be measured in place of only the animals' presence, in a non-destructive fashion.

\section{Sediment microtopography as a proxy for benthic activity}
\label{sec:review.proxy}
%=====================================================================
In this project, the case for microtopography as a proxy rests mainly on three studies by Schenone and colleagues:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
	\item In a multi-site field study, biogenic surface features predicted ecosystem functioning better than species density \citep{schenoneUnravelingEcosystemFunctioning2020};
	
	\item In flow-through laboratory tanks (\autoref{fig:schenonecustomincubationtank}), rugosity from a single 2D profile imaged through the tank wall explained 35\% to 61\,\% of the variability in oxygen and nutrient fluxes \citep{schenoneSedimentMicrotopographyPredicts2024};
	
	\item In the field, microtopography reconstructed with \gls{sfm} explained more than half of the variance in environmental variables, benthic fluxes, biodiversity and functional traits \citep{schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025}.
\end{itemize}
%These studies were subtidal, in New Zealand, with different species, and the relationships are correlative. 
None of these studies involved K.~kraussi. The two field studies, one intertidal \citep{schenoneUnravelingEcosystemFunctioning2020} and one subtidal \citep{schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025}, are correlative and establish microtopography as a surrogate rather than a mechanism. The laboratory study manipulated species composition and therefore supports a causal link \citep{schenoneSedimentMicrotopographyPredicts2024}, but only for two species under controlled conditions, with rugosity taken from a single 2D profile.There is still a knowledge gap on the link between structures produced by sandprawns and ecosystem functioning. Testing this relationship in Langebaan Lagoon is one of the aims of a wider study, that will be supported by accurate enough microtopography measurements that this project will contribute to.

How roughness is quantified matters as much as how it is measured. Traditional chain-and-tape rugosity compares the length of a chain laid along the bed with its straight-line length. It samples a single profile and overestimates rugosity when the chain wanders sideways \citep{friedmanMultiScaleMeasuresRugosity2012}. \citet{friedmanMultiScaleMeasuresRugosity2012} instead computed rugosity from stereo meshes relative to a plane of best fit (\autoref{fig:review.rugosity}), which decouples rugosity from slope, and SfM-derived rugosity has proved more powerful than chain-and-tape \citep{storlazziEndChainRugosity2016}. ISO 25178-2 standardises these quantities as areal parameters \citep{ISO2517822021,leachCharacterisationArealSurface2013}, where the developed interfacial area ratio $S_{dr}$ is rugosity expressed as a percentage excess area (\autoref{ch:theory}). \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022} used a comparable set of nine parameters from detrended depth images.

These parameters depend on how the surface is sampled. They vary with scan size and pixel resolution \citep{jacobsQuantitativeCharacterizationSurface2017}, and slope-based parameters such as $S_{dq}$, $S_{dr}$ and rugosity are dominated by the shortest resolved wavelengths, where sensor noise and smoothing also act. Rugosity from a depth camera is therefore only interpretable when the noise floor and effective resolution of the measurement are known.

\section{Non-contact measurement of sediment surfaces}

Coring and contact profiling damage the structures being measured \citep{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022}, so only non-contact methods are considered (\autoref{tab:review.methods}).

Sunlight tolerance therefore decides the choice of sensor. The D456 used here is the \gls{ip65}-rated variant of the D455 \citep{intel_d456}, which also suits wet intertidal work.

\section{Registration and fusion of multi-view depth scans}
\label{sec:review.fusion}
%=====================================================================
Fusing aligned range images into a weighted signed distance function averages noise, fills gaps and weights measurements by reliability \citep{curlessVolumetricMethodBuilding1996}. KinectFusion applied this in real time with a consumer depth camera and showed that the fused model contains detail absent from any single frame (\autoref{fig:review.kinectfusion}) \citep{newcombeKinectFusionRealtimeDense2011}. These methods were developed for objects and rooms, and judged by geometric accuracy rather than by roughness parameters.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\litfig{figs/ch2/kinectfusion_single_vs_fused.png}{0.7\linewidth}
	\caption{A Single Noisy Depth Frame (Left) Compared With the Surface Fused From Many Frames \citep{newcombeKinectFusionRealtimeDense2011}.}
	\label{fig:review.kinectfusion}
\end{figure}

Two results bear on sediment. First, nearly flat surfaces are degenerate for registration, since \gls{icp} cannot constrain sliding within a plane or rotation about its normal \citep{gelfandGeometricallyStableSampling2003}. A near-planar bed therefore needs external constraints such as AprilTags \citep{olsonAprilTagRobustFlexible2011,wangAprilTag2Efficient2016} or inertial measurements. Second, benthic fusion is normally done at survey scale using vehicle navigation \citep{friedmanMultiScaleMeasuresRugosity2012}, not by fusing repeated close-range views of one patch. \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022} used a single viewpoint, and the inherited system analysed each repeated scan individually. No study was found that fuses multi-view scans of a sediment patch and quantifies how the choice of views affects reconstruction accuracy and roughness.

\section{Summary and research gap}
\label{sec:review.gap}
%=====================================================================
\textit{K. kraussi} is a dominant ecosystem engineer of the Langebaan Lagoon intertidal space, and sediment microtopography is a credible proxy candidate for its bioturbation activity. Roughness parameters, however, depend on the noise and resolution of the measurement. Active stereo is the best-supported low-cost sensor for outdoor use, but it has not been tested in this setting. Four gaps remain, each addressed by one objective in \autoref{sec:ch1.projectobjectives} (\autoref{tab:review.gaps}).
