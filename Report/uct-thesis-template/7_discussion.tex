This chapter summarises the key findings of \autoref{ch:experiments} and \autoref{ch:Validation}, and relates them to the overarching project and the literature. Two findings recur throughout. First, reconstruction accuracy is set far more by geometry, meaning the working distance and the spread of viewpoints, than by the camera's software settings. Second, a single scan systematically overstates surface roughness, because its depth noise is measured as relief.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Camera Configuration}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Of the stream settings, resolution had by far the largest effect (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Res}). Moving from the default 848$\times$480 to 1280$\times$720 reduced the RMSE of the large egg box from 2.39\,mm to 2.07\,mm (13.4\,\%). Over the same change, completeness at 2\,mm rose from 47.5\,\% to 57.1\,\%. This follows from \autoref{eq:depthnoise}, since the focal length in pixels scales with resolution. The cost is that the minimum depth $Z_{\min}=fB/126$ also grows, to 0.52\,m at 1280$\times$720~\cite{intel_d456}.

The High Density preset performed best overall, with an egg box F-score of 59.2\,\% against 51.4\,\% for Default (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Preset}). High Accuracy gave among the lowest RMSE values (1.52 to 1.67\,mm), but at much lower completeness. Since fusion can fill holes from other views, High Accuracy combined with fusion warrants testing, which is discussed in \autoref{ch:furtherwork}.

The depth unit changed RMSE by at most 0.12\,mm (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Depth-Units}). This is expected, since even a 1\,mm quantisation step adds only about $1/\sqrt{12}\approx0.29$\,mm of noise, small compared with the 1.2 to 4.6\,mm of sensor noise measured on planes. The 0.1\,mm unit costs nothing within the working range and can help with small scale relief and slight planar trends, which is why it is still recommended.

Post-processing filters improved RMSE by at most 0.24\,mm (\autoref{fig:Filter-Comparison}). Raising the camera from 641\,mm to 933\,mm, by comparison, worsened RMSE by 1.13 to 1.24\,mm. Strong spatial smoothing was determined harmful, because it flattens the relief being measured. In the characterisation, filters were applied offline to recorded scans. Recording unfiltered data in the field and filtering afterwards therefore keeps every option open and reduces the capture processing load on the host device.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Working Distance, Viewing Angle and Lighting}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Working distance gave the most consistent trend in the report. Between 604\,mm and 903\,mm, the plane-fit RMS rose from 1.22\,mm to 4.58\,mm and the fraction of valid pixels fell from 99.6\,\% to 96.6\,\% (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Height}). On the reference objects, the fused large egg box fell from an F-score of 90.9\,\% at 641\,mm to 55.0\,\% at 933\,mm (\autoref{fig:Combination-Comparison}). This agrees with the stereo noise model of \citet{keselmanIntelRRealSenseTMStereoscopic2017} and with other depth cameras~\cite{mankoffKinectLowcostHighresolution2013,nguyenModelingKinectSensor2012}. Over this narrow range, however, the trend is near linear rather than quadratic.

Height therefore trades directly against coverage. The footprint grows with the square of height, so moving from 700\,mm to 933\,mm gives about 1.8 times the area. The D456 also cannot be lowered freely. Its 95\,mm baseline is designed for longer range~\cite{intel_d456,rustlerEmpiricalComparisonFour2025}, and tilting brings part of the view closer than the mount height. In initial experiments, depth anomalies appeared at 687\,mm with tilts of around 17\textdegree, although fused results at 641\,mm were still the best of any height. A height of about 700\,mm is therefore recommended for accuracy. The maximum height should be used where area is needed, with at least one patch scanned at both heights so that height-dependent noise can be quantified.

Tilt degraded planar accuracy, with the plane-fit RMS rising from 1.62\,mm to 2.29\,mm at 28\textdegree{} pitch (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Angle}). On the 3D objects, by contrast, RMSE and completeness were generally best at the extreme tilts (\autoref{fig:Accuracy-Vs-Tilt} and \autoref{fig:Completeness-Vs-Tilt}). The two results are consistent. On a plane, tilt only increases obliquity, which raises noise~\cite{nguyenModelingKinectSensor2012}. On relief, tilt turns some slopes towards the camera at the expense of others. Each view therefore sees a different part of the surface well, which motivates fusion.

Ambient light raised the plane-fit RMS from 1.80\,mm in darkness to 4.43\,mm at 151\,lx (\autoref{fig:Scatter-Metric-Vs-Light}). Daylight was more damaging per lux than artificial light. The featureless board failed in sunlight, whereas textured ground returned complete data at 9\,800\,lx. This is the expected behaviour of active stereo, which degrades towards passive stereo on textured surfaces~\cite{keselmanIntelRRealSenseTMStereoscopic2017}. It agrees with outdoor tests of the D435~\cite{vitComparingRGBDSensors2018} and extends the indoor-only evaluation of~\cite{halmetschlager-funekEmpiricalEvaluationTen2019}. The specular noise observed on wet flat sand (\autoref{sec:rugosity}) shows that water films remain a risk. The shading tarp should therefore be kept.

\section{Fusion and Roughness Estimation}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Fusing all views improved every metric at every height (\autoref{fig:Combination-Comparison}). For the large egg box, RMSE fell by 0.61\,mm on average and the F-score rose by 14.0 percentage points. For the small egg box, RMSE fell by 0.25\,mm and the F-score rose by 5.8 points. The fused surface is visibly smoother (\autoref{fig:smallwave701mmfusedside} and \autoref{fig:smallwave701mmsingleside}).

This extends the established benefits of fusion~\cite{curlessVolumetricMethodBuilding1996,newcombeKinectFusionRealtimeDense2011} to close-range sediment-like relief. That was one of the gaps identified in \autoref{sec:review.gap}. On the small egg box, the gain is limited because its 25\,mm features approach the camera's resolution and the 10\,mm MLS radius. Fusion cannot recover relief that no single scan resolves.

The number of view points also matters less than their spread (\autoref{fig:Combination-Accuracy}). Four rotations at tilts of 0\textdegree{} and $\pm$25\textdegree{} (12 scans) matched the full 44-view sweep, with an F-score of 0.73 in both cases. This makes multi-view capture feasible within a low-tide window.

On the reference objects, single scans gave rugosity closer to the reference than fused scans. With planar detrending, the large egg box gave 1.31 for the single scan, 1.25 for the fused scan and 1.34 for the reference (\autoref{tab:Big_Egg_Rugosity}). This result is misleading as every reconstruction under-represents relief amplitude, as the negative signed bias shows. Rugosity scales with the squared slope ($S_{dr}\approx 50\,S_{dq}^2\,\%$), so this loss is amplified, most strongly on the steep small egg box (1.26 against 1.69). The single scan scores higher only because its noise adds slope variance that partly cancels the lost amplitude.

The laboratory sand confirms this. On flat dry sand, which should have a rugosity of 1, the fused scan gave 1.0034 and the single scan 1.0193 (\autoref{tab:Sand_Rugosity}). On shaped sand, single scans added 2.1 to 2.6 percentage points of excess area. This is roughly a third of the excess area that the fused scans measured on the same surfaces. The ± values in \autoref{tab:Sand_Rugosity} reflect processing options rather than repeat captures, so repeatability remains unknown.

Planar and quadratic detrending preserved the reference relief, whereas higher orders removed part of it. This agrees with \citet{azharRGBDFrameworkCapturing2022}. As \citet{jacobsQuantitativeCharacterizationSurface2017} caution, rugosity is therefore only comparable between scans with the same height, grid spacing, smoothing, detrending and views. It should also be referenced to a flat-surface noise floor.

\section{Implications for the Overarching Project}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Microtopography has explained a substantial share of benthic fluxes and biodiversity~\cite{schenoneSedimentMicrotopographyPredicts2024,schenoneSeafloorSedimentMicrotopography2025}. It is only a useful proxy, however, if measured differences come from the sediment rather than the instrument. This project shows that single scans, or changes in height or processing between surveys, introduce instrument differences of the same order as the surface signal.

Fused scans captured with a fixed protocol make these effects small and consistent. That protocol is 1280$\times$720 resolution, the High Density preset, a height of about 700\,mm, four rotations at 0\textdegree{} and $\pm$25\textdegree, and fixed processing settings. The large egg box, the closest analogue to the 3 to 5\,cm high mounds of thalassinidean shrimps~\cite{inbook}, was reconstructed best, with a fused F-score of 90.9\,\%. Finer features such as burrow openings remain near the system's limit. The main limitations are single trials per condition, uncalibrated light readings and the absence of field testing.
